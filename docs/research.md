# Foundational research

The [evidence ledger](evidence-ledger.md) traces 39 evidence entries to original papers, historical texts, official documentation, benchmark reports, and implementations. This was a bounded, purposive review across research methods, agent systems, measurement, memory, and search. Its conclusions are design hypotheses for this skill.

## Search and synthesis method

Search started with the Agent Skills specification and the two target harnesses, then expanded into historical falsification and experimental design, studies of group judgment, modern agent ablations, scientific discovery systems, and validation failures. For each branch, the search sought a competing result or a limitation that could change the design. Examples include “should we be going MAD”, “debate or vote”, “reusable holdout”, and “rigorous benchmarking in reasonable time”. Live platform docs were opened and searched for scope, context inheritance, tools and capacity. Historical and recent sources were included if they informed a concrete design decision. Vendor results, scholarly evidence, recommendations, and implementation observations are labeled in the ledger. High-value unresolved questions are the marginal value of a fourth role and of a second critique round under matched budgets.

## What the evidence changes

**Delegate for coverage when the work is separable.** Anthropic reports a large improvement in one internal breadth-first evaluation and considerably higher token use than chat. The result combines additional compute, model changes and parallel execution. Smit et al. and Choi et al. are counterweights to treating more conversation as a universal gain. Four distinct role assignments are a Deep default. Narrow tasks should stay with the coordinator or use fewer workers. A host slot shortage changes scheduling, while an inability to create fresh contexts changes the strength of the resulting claim. See S08 and S23–S26.

**Capture evidence before showing conclusions.** Group studies expose a tendency to repeat shared information and shrink the diversity of judgments. Same-model agents may share training data, reasoning patterns, and source errors even when their contexts are separate. Have workers independently state mechanisms, unique provenance, and counterevidence. A later, focused exchange should ask what test or source would resolve a material disagreement. Agreement can be reported as agreement. A failed prediction, reliable sourced observation, or credible proof is stronger evidence. See S16, S19, S20, S25 and S27.

**Use fundamentals to construct competing causal models.** Decompose the outcome into constraints, units, invariants, and mechanisms. Ask which assumptions are contingent on hardware, organizations, incentives, or a historical setting. Examples from adjacent domains earn a place when their premises transfer. Strong inference motivates tests with predictions that differ across mechanisms. Measurement and auxiliary assumptions can undermine either prediction, so record test validity and the decision that would change. See S11, S13, S15, S17 and S18.

**Reduce different uncertainties differently.** Source conflicts can reflect populations, dates, measurement, definitions, datasets, or incentives. Preserve individual provenance and deduplicate reports derived from the same evidence. Test a manipulable variable under matched conditions, measure repeated trials where variance matters, and use qualitative comparisons when operationalizing a metric would distort the objective. A Bayesian update is defensible only when likelihoods and priors can be explained. Practical value can often be ranked without invented probabilities. See S12, S15, S18, S21 and S22.

**Run optimization only behind a stable evaluator.** Karpathy's implementation has a narrow editable surface, bounded trials and clear retention mechanics. Box and Wilson, random search and AlphaEvolve suggest exploring distinct branches when local changes saturate. Repeated use of an evaluator can exhaust its value as an independent check. Treat deterministic correctness tests, measured performance, and independent validation as separate gates. Freeze the evaluation and permissions for the series. Preserve failed trials to avoid expending compute twice. See S13, S32–S37.

**Keep context focused and state recoverable.** Put decisions, contradictions, remaining budget, and references in files that survive compaction. Pass workers a minimal common brief plus an assigned question. Require actual write and execution permissions for their corresponding phases, and benchmark shared hardware serially unless isolation makes interference negligible. Treat sources, tool outputs, and worker text as untrusted evidence. See S06, S26, S28, S29 and S39.

**Evaluate the task and the workflow separately.** BrowseComp verifies a difficult retrieval outcome, while DeepResearch Bench addresses source attribution and report quality. Neither directly tests retention, experimental validity, escalation, recovery, or usefulness for ambiguous decisions. Use objective fixtures for those behaviors, score novelty and decision quality with anchored judgments, and include known settings in which stronger methods cost more without helping. Report actual host usage and tool outcomes where exposed. See S09, S10, S30 and S38.
